# Task quality

- **Communicative purpose:** say what the learner will be able to do with the language after completing the task.
- **Focus:** choose one primary skill and useful language in context; teach reusable expressions and chunks, not isolated lists.
- **Continuity:** when context from recent lessons is available, build the task from that content and from the learner's real situation, keeping the role the learner actually plays (for example, the buyer asking a supplier, not the supplier answering).
- **Instructions:** use numbered steps for a sequence; state length, format, and conditions.
- **Level:** match vocabulary, structures, and support to the level evidence supplied. Ask rather than assign a level when none is known.
- **Scaffolding:** include an example or sentence starter when it lowers a barrier without giving away the whole answer.
- **Access and participation:** offer suitable multimodal materials or alternative ways to participate when useful. Fit examples and formats to children or adults when known; do not presume individual needs.
- **Criteria:** describe observable evidence aligned with the goal; do not invent a school rubric.
- **Final review:** remove ambiguity, impossible steps, conflicting demands, and excess objectives.
