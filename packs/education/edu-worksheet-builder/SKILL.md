# Worksheet builder

Build practice students can do alone, with a key the teacher can trust.

## Ask first

1. Grade, topic, and the objective the practice serves.
2. Number of items or minutes.
3. Format: print, digital, or both. Whether students may use calculators, notes, or texts.
4. Whether to make a supported or extended version too.

## Build it

1. Header: title, name and date lines, and the objective in student language.
2. Directions: one short sentence per task, at or below the grade's reading level. Bold the verb ("**Circle** the...").
3. Order items from easier to harder. Start with one worked example.
4. Mix item types where it fits: short answer, fill in, matching, diagrams, one open question that asks students to explain.
5. Tag each item with its DOK level in the teacher copy only.
6. Leave enough answer space for the age group's handwriting.
7. Use names, places, and contexts that are varied and neutral. Avoid brand names and stereotypes.

## Answer key

- Give the answer to every item. For multistep math, show the steps.
- For open items, give a model answer and what a full-credit answer must include.
- Work every item yourself before you write the key. If two answers are defensible, say so.

## Versions

When the teacher asks for versions, keep the same objective and item count. The `edu-differentiation` skill explains which supports and extensions to use.

## Output

The student copy, then a page break marker (`---`), then the teacher answer key.

## Guardrails

- Standards: use only the codes the teacher gives you. If there are none, write `[standard: ask teacher]`. Never invent a code or quote standard text from memory.
- Privacy: work without student names or identifying details. If the teacher pastes them, replace them with "Student A" or initials and say that you did. Ask for the accommodation a student needs, not for the IEP or 504 document.
- Age and safety: fit reading level, content, and examples to the grade. Flag sensitive topics, lab or physical safety, and anything the school may need to approve.
- Accuracy: work every answer key yourself. Mark any fact you are not sure of with `[verify]`.
- The teacher decides: you draft. Grades, placements, and IEP services are the teacher's and the school's call.
