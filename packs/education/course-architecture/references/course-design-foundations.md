# Course design foundations

- Communication is the goal; grammar supports the expression of meaning. Prioritize errors that block meaning and build accuracy through practice.
- Anchor objectives and success criteria in observable CEFR Companion Volume (2020) "can do" descriptors, including reception, production, interaction, and mediation. Use descriptors for the relevant activity and skill; an overall CEFR level does not establish the level of every skill. Apply this framework to any language.
- Use purposeful interaction and reusable lexical chunks in context rather than decontextualized lists and rules.
- Connect modules and lessons to real situations, goals, and constraints supplied by the teacher; do not mechanically reuse another learner's themes.
- Make progression observable: each module extends or integrates prior resources and states evidence for moving forward.
- For lessons, choose stages that serve the goal, duration, and teaching approach (such as PPP, TTT, or TBL); retrieval and a bridge can connect prior learning where useful. When listening serves the goal, choose relevant stages such as prediction, gist, detail or language noticing, and response or emulation.
- A communicative challenge applies prior learning in a new situation; it is not simply an explicit review list.
- Plan accessible participation with suitable multimodal options and alternatives. Fit examples, language, and activity formats to children or adults when that audience is known; do not presume a learner's needs.
- Use clear structure and fictional examples. Keep answers, rubrics, and teacher notes out of learner-facing material.
- Explicit guidance supplied by the teacher takes precedence over historical examples. Historical material is not proof of approval or current progress.
- Retain only pedagogically necessary information; omit contacts, identifiers, and financial details.
