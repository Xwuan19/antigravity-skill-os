# Item-writing rules

Rules for writing fair test items. Based on widely used guidance for classroom assessment, such as Haladyna, Downing, and Rodriguez (2002), "A Review of Multiple-Choice Item-Writing Guidelines for Classroom Assessment".

## Content

- Test one objective per item.
- Test important content, not trivia.
- Keep each item independent. One item must not give away another item's answer.
- Use new examples, not sentences copied from the textbook, so the item tests understanding instead of recall of wording.

## Stems

- Write the stem as a full question or a clear incomplete statement.
- Put most of the words in the stem, not in the options.
- Avoid negatives. If you must use one, write it as **NOT** or **EXCEPT**.
- Remove clues: grammar that matches only one option, or a word repeated from the stem in the correct option.

## Options

- Write three or four options. Three good options beat four with a weak one.
- Make every distractor plausible to a student with a specific misconception.
- Keep options similar in length, grammar, and detail.
- Order numeric options from smallest to largest. Order other options logically or at random.
- Avoid "all of the above", "none of the above", and "both A and C".

## Constructed response

- Tell students how long the answer should be and what it must include.
- Write the scoring guide before you give the item.
- Score the content asked for, not spelling or handwriting, unless writing quality is the objective.

## Accessibility

- Keep the reading level below the grade level of the content, unless reading is what you test.
- Use clear fonts, enough white space, and diagrams with labels.
- Don't rely on color alone.
