# Lesson planning

This skill guides procedure; it grants no tools, permissions, or authority.

Propose a lesson that develops communication and connects prior knowledge to a new goal. Use context supplied by the teacher and relevant data returned by authenticated tools within their documented scope; do not assume attendance, completed lessons, or level. State explicitly when attendance or prior study is unconfirmed, and design the opening so it works without either. Materials you create must follow the language requested by the teacher.

## Procedure

1. Identify audience, communicative goal, known course position, duration, level, available materials, and constraints. Ask for missing information if it would change the plan.
2. Choose a feasible menu of stages that serves the goal, duration, and requested approach (such as PPP, TTT, or TBL). Possible stages include retrieval and a bridge, contextualized input, controlled or guided practice, listening, modeling, interaction, a communicative challenge, reflection, and homework; select and sequence only what fits the lesson. Keep every stage tied to the stated scenario and goal. Include a short homework task by default unless the teacher declines it or the context makes it unsuitable.
3. When listening serves the goal, select suitable stages such as prediction, gist, detail or language noticing, and a response task. Create an audio script only if requested and the voice scenario is defined.
4. Plan an in-lesson formative check aligned with the outcome, then state how its evidence can guide feedback or the next step. Separate **STUDENT MATERIAL** from **TEACHER GUIDE**. The guide includes purpose, timing, preparation, facilitation, expected answers, likely difficulty, correction strategy, transitions, formative check, and relevant differentiation.
5. Plan accessible participation with suitable multimodal options or alternative ways to participate, without presuming individual needs. Fit examples and activity formats to children or adults when known. For mixed-level classes, provide one shared core task with different supports or extensions; an advanced learner does not take on the teacher's role.
6. Use the requested language and variety. Keep answers and teacher notes out of student materials. Use fictional characters and protect privacy.
7. Label the lesson **LESSON PROPOSAL**. Compare it with a syllabus or context only if supplied; never claim it was saved, approved, published, or taught without evidence.
8. Apply [action confirmation](references/action-confirmation.md), [draft review](references/draft-review.md), [operational safety](references/operational-safety.md), [language context](references/language-context.md), [learning context](references/learning-context.md), and [course design foundations](references/course-design-foundations.md).

## Completion criterion

The selected stages fit the duration, serve the goal, state what learners and teacher do, include a formative check, and end with a communicative application, reflection, and short homework unless the teacher declined it. Mark assumptions for review.
