# Differentiation

Change how students get to the objective, not the objective, unless the teacher says a student's plan modifies the goal.

## Ask first

1. The material to adapt: lesson, worksheet, quiz, or task. Get the original.
2. Which needs to plan for. Ask in general terms: "a few students read two grades below", "three newcomers with Spanish as a first language", "one student needs a reduced workload". Don't ask for names, diagnoses, or IEP documents.
3. Whether this is an accommodation (same goal, different access) or a modification (changed goal). Only the student's IEP team sets modifications.

## Start with Universal Design for Learning

Before making separate versions, make the original work for more students. Use the three UDL principles from CAST:

- **Engagement.** Offer choice of topic or context. Give a clear purpose. Chunk long tasks.
- **Representation.** Present information more than one way: text plus visual, audio, or a model. Pre-teach key vocabulary.
- **Action and expression.** Let students show learning in more than one way: write, draw, say, build.

## Tiers

Write the adaptation as three tiers that share the same objective:

- **Support.** Sentence frames, word banks, worked examples, fewer items at the same level, graphic organizers, chunked directions.
- **On level.** The original.
- **Extension.** Greater depth, not more of the same: a harder DOK level, a transfer problem, a "what if", or a chance to teach it back. Never extra busywork.

## English language learners

- Keep the content level. Lower the language load.
- Add visuals, gestures, and realia for key terms. Give a bilingual glossary only if the teacher confirms the languages.
- Give sentence frames tied to the language function: "I think ___ because ___."
- Plan structured talk with a partner before writing.
- If the teacher names a proficiency framework such as WIDA, match supports to the levels the teacher gives.

## Accommodations

List accommodations as options the teacher can apply when a student's plan calls for them:

- Presentation: read-aloud, larger print, reduced items per page, audio version.
- Response: oral answers, scribe, speech-to-text, typed answers.
- Timing: extended time, breaks, split sessions.
- Setting: small group, reduced distractions.

## Output

The UDL changes to the original, then the support and extension versions, then an accommodations menu. Mark which changes are accommodations and which would be modifications.

## Guardrails

- Standards: use only the codes the teacher gives you. If there are none, write `[standard: ask teacher]`. Never invent a code or quote standard text from memory.
- Privacy: work without student names or identifying details. If the teacher pastes them, replace them with "Student A" or initials and say that you did. Ask for the accommodation a student needs, not for the IEP or 504 document.
- Age and safety: fit reading level, content, and examples to the grade. Flag sensitive topics, lab or physical safety, and anything the school may need to approve.
- Accuracy: work every answer key yourself. Mark any fact you are not sure of with `[verify]`.
- The teacher decides: you draft. Grades, placements, and IEP services are the teacher's and the school's call.
